# Navigate — the method for entering unknown domains

The problem this solves: being dropped into a domain you don't know, unable to name the gaps, unable to say what you want, unable to ask the right questions. "Not knowing what you don't know."

The method turns that into "I know what I don't know" — which is a normal, solvable situation.

## The five moves

1. **Ask for the questions first.** Before anything else, ask an expert or the AI: *"what should I be asking about this?"* The gaps you don't know exist are exactly the things you won't think to ask about. One question surfaces them.

2. **Build the domain map.** One focused session (60-90 minutes), saved to `maps/[domain].md`:
   - The core vocabulary — roughly 30 terms with plain meanings. Words are how you want things and how you understand answers.
   - The landscape — the parts, the tools, who does what.
   - The failure modes — how things in this domain usually go wrong.
   - The questions people in the field ask.
   The map is the bridge from unknown to known. You don't learn the field; you get its skeleton.

3. **Point, don't describe.** Collect 5-10 reference examples of "good" in the domain. When words fail, examples carry meaning: "like X, not like Y." One reference beats twenty minutes of fumbling for the right phrase.

4. **Checklists replace judgment.** Every field has standard checklists, heuristics, or best-practice lists. A newcomer running them finds real problems — you don't need expertise to run a checklist. Experts are just people with internalized checklists.

5. **Say what you want in the map's words.** Vocabulary converts feelings into requests. "It looks off" becomes "the spacing is inconsistent and nothing has visual priority" — a sentence any professional can act on.

## Rules of the method

- Never start a domain by trying to learn the whole thing. Map first, learn as needed.
- Every new domain gets its own map file. Maps accumulate; each new domain is cheaper than the last.
- The map is a working tool, not homework. It lives in the vault and gets carried into the work.
- When the map is wrong or missing something, fix it then — maps stay live.

## Map template

Each map in `maps/[domain].md` follows the same shape:

1. Vocabulary — the ~30 words with plain meanings
2. Landscape — parts, tools, who does what
3. Failure modes — how things usually go wrong
4. Questions to ask — the field's questions, and what to ask first
5. Checklists — the standard lists that replace judgment
6. Reference gallery — examples of "good," collected as you find them
